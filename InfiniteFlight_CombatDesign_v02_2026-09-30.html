A modern JetFighter, built into InfiniteFlight

This is a design for a browser combat mode that keeps what made the JetFighter series fun in the 1990s and fixes what reviewers complained about. It runs in Three.js, it works with your SideWinder, and it's playable on aiquorum.org.

The one-line pitch: fast, low, over real ground, from a carrier you actually have to trap on.

Every JetFighter review praised the same thing, which was the feeling of speed near the ground. JetFighter IV was set over the San Francisco Bay Area and Lake Tahoe. InfiniteFlight already has SF Bay and real USGS Yosemite terrain, so the home theater is already half built.

Design pillars

Eight rules, each traced to a review

1. Speed near the ground

Dense ground detail, roads and rivers, strong motion cues and buffeting make 500 knots at 300 feet feel fast.

Every JF3 and Full Burn review praised this.

2. Fights at visual range

The enemy AI pulls you into turning fights. Missiles matter, but a gun kill is the fight people remember.

GameSpot liked JF3's close-in AI.

3. A carrier you have to earn

You get a catapult launch, a moving deck, a working meatball, LSO radio calls, a graded trap and bolters.

JF3 had the LSO only in its manual's story.

4. Every jet feels different

Each aircraft gets its own turn rate, energy loss, g limit and slow-speed manners.

All three later games were criticized for this.

5. A campaign that reacts

Missions branch on what you hit and what you lose, and enemy flights spawn differently each time.

JF IV's repeated scripted spawns were panned.

6. Assists you choose

Each realism setting is a separate switch, from auto-trap to full blackout and fuel limits.

JF3 did this well with its "Ease" options.

7. Get flying in one click

Instant action and a skip-the-flight-home option come standard. There's no walk-around carrier menu.

GameSpot called JF3's carrier menu a nuisance.

8. Other people

AIQuorum leaderboards and ghost replays come first, then live dogfights and a mission editor.

Multiplayer was the most missed feature.
Try it: the carrier approach

The meatball and the LSO

On a real carrier approach you fly a glide slope of about 3.5 degrees at a fixed angle of attack. You watch an amber light, the "ball", against a row of green reference lights. Drag the slider to fly the approach high or low and see what the pilot sees and what the LSO says.

LowOn glide slopeHigh
LSO on the radio
"Roger ball."
Likely grade: OK, 3-wire

The ball is level with the green lights, so you're on the glide slope. Hold your angle of attack and don't flare. JetFighter III's manual said the same thing: "literally fly right into the deck!"

This is simplified. In the game the ball will respond to your real height, the deck will pitch in the swell, and the LSO will grade every pass on a boarding board.

Cockpit

The combat HUD

The HUD keeps JetFighter's simple approach so you can concentrate on flying. It shows the few things a fighter pilot needs: speed, altitude, heading, g, the target box and a gun funnel.

This is a mockup. The dashed lines are the gun funnel, which shows where your bullets will be when they reach the target's range.

Content

Aircraft, weapons and missions

Aircraft you fly

  • The F/A-18E Super Hornet is the carrier workhorse and the best first jet.
  • The F-14 Tomcat is already in the sim and gets swing wings and the Phoenix missile.
  • The F-35C is the modern stealth jet and flies from the carrier.
  • The F-22 flies from airfields and has thrust vectoring and supercruise, like JF3's F-22N.
  • The F-117 is already in the sim and handles night strike missions.

What you fight

  • Enemy fighters include the MiG-29, Su-27, Su-35, Su-57 and J-20, each with its own AI skill level.
  • Ground threats include flak guns, SAM sites and ships that shoot back.
  • Targets include airfields, bridges, radar sites, convoys and a carrier group.

How we'd build it

Build order

Each step is playable on its own, so you can fly and give feedback after every one. The session counts are my estimates.

1

Flight model and the stick (2 to 3 sessions)

This is Phase 2 of the current plan: angle of attack, stall, g limits, blackout, and a different envelope for each jet. It comes first because every later step depends on it.

2

The carrier (2 to 3 sessions)

This adds a moving carrier off the Golden Gate, catapult launches, the meatball, the arresting wires, bolters, LSO calls and grades.

3

Sound (1 session)

This adds the engine, wind rush, the gun, missile launches, radio calls, and the warning tone when an enemy radar locks you.

4

Guns-only dogfight (2 to 3 sessions)

This adds one enemy jet with a turning-fight AI, bullets, hits, damage and a padlock view.

5

Missiles, radar and countermeasures (2 sessions)

This adds a radar display, lock-on, Sidewinder and AMRAAM guidance, chaff and flares, and wingmen with attack, follow and hold commands.

6

Strike missions (2 sessions)

This adds bombs with CCIP, ground targets, flak and SAM sites.

7

Campaign and leaderboards (3 sessions)

This adds branching missions, the moving front line, AIQuorum scores and ghost replays.

8

Live multiplayer and a mission editor (later)

This needs a WebSocket server on AIQuorum, so it waits until the rest is proven.
